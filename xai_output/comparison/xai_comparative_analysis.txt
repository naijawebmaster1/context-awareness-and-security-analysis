║         Liveness Detection — CASIA-FASD Dataset                 ║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝

Four explainability methods — Grad-CAM, SHAP, LIME, and SCOUTER —
were applied to the trained LivenessNet MLP classifier using the
nn_lpq feature extraction mode on the CASIA-FASD dataset.

The central question this analysis answers is:

  Does the model detect genuine physiological traits of a live
  face, or does it exploit spurious contextual biases present
  in the CASIA-FASD dataset?

SECTION 1 — XAI METHODS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

1.1  GRAD-CAM  (Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
What it extracts:
  Grad-CAM computes the gradient of the output with respect to the
  last hidden layer (32 units), alpha-weights activations by those
  gradients, and back-projects the result through the MLP weight
  matrices (W6→W3→W0) to the input space.  For nn_lpq the input is
  a 256-dimensional LPQ histogram; the result is a 256-element
  importance bar chart showing which phase-texture bins drove the
  live/spoof decision at the final hidden layer.

Advantages:
  + Fast — single forward + backward pass, no extra model queries.
  + Faithful — uses actual model gradients, not a surrogate.
  + Deterministic — same input always produces the same map.
  + Captures which LPQ bins the penultimate representation relied on.

Drawbacks:
  - MLP adaptation requires manual weight-chain back-projection;
    the back-projection mixes all 256 bins via the weight matrices,
    making the importance bar chart diffuse.
  - Only reflects the last hidden layer; early-layer specialisation
    is not directly visible.
  - For LPQ histogram inputs there is no spatial structure to
    recover — the 256 bins are global phase-texture counts, not
    pixels, so spatial localisation is impossible by design.

1.2  SHAP  (SHapley Additive exPlanations — KernelSHAP)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
What it extracts:
  SHAP assigns each of the 256 LPQ bins a Shapley value — its
  average marginal contribution to the output across all possible
  feature coalitions, measured against a background distribution
  of 100 training samples.  Positive values push the prediction
  toward spoof; negative values toward live (since P(live) is the
  explained quantity).

Advantages:
  + Theoretically grounded: satisfies efficiency, symmetry, dummy,
    and additivity axioms from cooperative game theory.
  + Model-agnostic — works with any black-box callable.
  + Signed values distinguish bins that help vs. hurt liveness.
  + Can aggregate over many samples for global bin importance.

Drawbacks:
  - Slowest method — KernelSHAP runs 200 coalition evaluations per
    sample, making it impractical at scale without approximation.
  - Results depend on the background distribution; a biased baseline
    leads to biased attributions.
  - Assumes bin independence within coalitions, which may not hold
    since adjacent LPQ bins often co-occur in correlated textures.

1.3  LIME  (Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
What it extracts:
  LIME perturbs the input LPQ histogram 1,000 times in its
  neighbourhood (Gaussian noise around discretised bin values),
  queries the MLP on each perturbed histogram, then fits a sparse
  weighted linear model to the query results.  The top-50 linear
  coefficients reveal which LPQ bins a locally linear approximation
  of the MLP relies on near that specific sample.

Advantages:
  + Model-agnostic — treats the MLP as a pure black box.
  + Intuitive output: linear weights over named bins are easy to
    communicate and tie directly to phase-texture semantics.
  + Moderate speed — faster than SHAP for 256-dim histogram data.
  + Flexible: the same framework applies to any tabular descriptor.

Drawbacks:
  - Locally faithful only — the linear surrogate may break down
    outside the immediate neighbourhood of the sample.
  - Non-deterministic — different random seeds yield different
    neighbourhood samples and therefore different weight values.
  - Neighbourhood perturbation (Gaussian on discretised bins) may
    not preserve the normalisation constraint of the histogram,
    generating unrealistic perturbed inputs.
  - Can miss complex non-linear bin interactions that Grad-CAM
    and SHAP capture more directly through model internals.

1.4  SCOUTER  (Slot Attention-based Classifier for Explainable Recognition)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
What it extracts:
  SCOUTER replaces the MLP classification head with a slot attention
  module.  Two positive slots and two negative slots compete over the
  256-bin LPQ histogram input.  Positive slots learn to attend to
  bins that support a live prediction; negative slots attend to
  bins that support a spoof prediction.  The live logit is the
  mean positive-slot score minus the mean negative-slot score.  No
  backward pass is needed at explanation time — the attention weights
  stored during the forward pass ARE the explanation.  The net
  evidence bar chart (positive attention minus negative attention)
  over the 256 LPQ bins is the final explanation.

Advantages:
  + Intrinsic — explanations are part of the model, not post-hoc.
  + No extra inference cost: explanations come free with prediction.
  + Separate positive/negative maps make the evidence structure
    explicit: which bins support live AND which support spoof.
  + Deterministic at inference time (stochastic only during training).

Drawbacks:
  - Requires training a second classifier; performance may differ
    from the original MLP.
  - Slot attention is stochastic at training time (random slot init),
    so different runs may partition live/spoof evidence differently
    across bins.
  - For LPQ histogram inputs the 256 bins have no spatial adjacency;
    the slot attention mechanism cannot exploit local bin structure.

SECTION 3 — PHYSIOLOGICAL TRAITS VS. CONTEXTUAL BIAS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

This section is the core deliverable of the XAI analysis.  Each
method is used not merely to produce a bar chart but to answer a
specific diagnostic question about what the model has actually
learned to detect.

LPQ Feature Background
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  nn_lpq extracts a 256-bin normalised histogram from the full
  image after Single-Scale Retinex preprocessing.  For each pixel
  a 7×7 window is analysed by 8 complex quadrature filters; the
  signs of the 8 filter responses are combined into an 8-bit code
  (0–255).  The histogram counts how often each of the 256 phase-
  texture pattern codes appears across the entire image.

  This is a GLOBAL statistic — each bin represents a texture
  pattern type, not a spatial location.  There is no 64×64 grid
  and no periocular region.  XAI outputs for nn_lpq are always
  bar charts over 256 bins, never spatial heatmaps.

  LPQ bin semantics:
    • Bin 0   (0b00000000): all 8 filter responses are negative
                            → smooth, slowly varying image regions
    • Bin 255 (0b11111111): all 8 filter responses are positive
                            → maximally complex phase patterns
    • Mid-range bins       : mixed responses → intermediate texture
    • High-popcount bins   : codes with many active bits → multi-
                            directional phase activity, characteristic
                            of irregular surfaces such as skin pores
                            and fine wrinkles

── What "physiological traits" looks like in an LPQ bar chart ──

  A model that has learned genuine anti-spoofing cues should show:
  • High importance concentrated in LPQ bins that encode the
    material difference between live skin and reproduction media.
  • Live skin has complex, three-dimensional microgeometry (pores,
    fine wrinkles, subsurface scattering) that activates a wide
    spread of mid-to-high complexity phase-texture codes.
  • Print attacks (halftone dots, paper surface) produce smoother,
    more uniform patches concentrated in low-to-mid complexity bins;
    screen replay attacks (pixel grid, Moiré) generate their own
    characteristic periodic bin signature.
  • A physiologically grounded model's spoof-supporting bins (red
    in SHAP, purple in LIME) should correspond to codes that appear
    more frequently in spoofed images than in live ones — for example,
    smooth-region codes that reflect the flat surface of a printed
    photo or the uniform background of a replayed video.
  • Conversely, live-supporting bins (blue in SHAP, green in LIME)
    should correspond to codes that are more frequent in genuine
    skin texture — irregular, multi-directional phase patterns with
    high bit-count codes.

── What "contextual bias" looks like in an LPQ bar chart ───────

  A model that has overfit to spurious dataset correlations shows:
  • High importance on extreme bins near 0 (perfectly smooth,
    dominated by background and bright overexposed areas) or near
    255 (maximally complex, dominated by noise and frame edges).
    Both are strongly influenced by camera settings, recording
    distance, and background colour rather than face material.
  • Diffuse, flat importance spread uniformly across all 256 bins,
    indicating the model responds to the overall histogram shape
    rather than to specific discriminative phase-texture codes.
  • Importance concentrated in bins that vary with recording-session
    conditions: lighting angle shifts the smooth-region (bin 0)
    count; subject distance changes the dominant texture scale and
    which mid-range bins fire; camera model differences create
    different noise spectra that map to different bins.
  • Inconsistent important bin patterns across samples from the
    same class: two genuine live faces show completely different
    important bins, suggesting the model reacts to per-subject or
    per-session statistics rather than a universal skin signature.

── Grad-CAM findings ───────────────────────────────────────────

  Grad-CAM back-projects from the last hidden layer (32 units)
  through the MLP weight chain to produce a 256-element importance
  bar chart over the LPQ bins.  Because the fully connected layers
  mix all bins during the forward pass, the back-projected vector
  tends to be diffuse, highlighting many bins at similar magnitudes.

  For correctly classified SPOOF samples (P(live) ≈ 0.00):
    The bar chart shows moderate spread of importance with slightly
    elevated weights on low-complexity bins (codes 0–63).  This is
    consistent with the model detecting that spoof images contain
    a higher proportion of smooth, low-variation regions — the
    pattern produced by the flat surface of printed paper or the
    static uniform background of a replayed video.  However, because
    Grad-CAM back-projection through multiple fully connected layers
    is inherently diffuse, this signal is not sharply localised to
    a narrow bin range, making it difficult to attribute the decision
    to a specific phase-texture code rather than a holistic histogram
    shape.

  For misclassified LIVE samples (P(live) ≈ 0.07–0.20, true=live):
    The importance bar chart is nearly flat across all 256 bins,
    with no concentrated peaks.  This indicates the model's error
    is distributional: no individual LPQ bin is responsible for
    the misclassification.  Instead, the model's live/spoof boundary
    in 256-dimensional LPQ space does not accommodate this subject's
    overall phase-texture histogram, which may differ from the
    training distribution due to subject-specific skin tone, camera
    distance, or lighting conditions — none of which are face-
    liveness signals.  This is a hallmark of contextual bias.

  VERDICT (Grad-CAM): Mixed evidence.  Spoof classifications show
  modest elevation in low-complexity bins consistent with smoother
  reproduction-medium texture.  Live-face errors are distributional
  with no discriminative bin structure.  Contextual bias is the
  dominant pattern, but a weak physiological signal is present in
  the spoof direction.

── SHAP findings ───────────────────────────────────────────────

  KernelSHAP provides signed attributions against a 100-sample
  training background across all 256 LPQ bins, revealing which
  specific phase-texture codes shift the prediction away from
  the baseline and in which direction.

  For correctly classified SPOOF samples:
    Spoof-supporting positive Shapley values (red bars) appear
    predominantly in the low-complexity bin range (codes 0–63),
    which correspond to smooth, slowly varying texture regions.
    This is the clearest physiological signal: spoofed faces
    (printed photos, screen replays) contain large smooth areas —
    flat paper surface, uniform background in a replayed video —
    that generate many low-complexity LPQ codes.  Genuine live
    faces by contrast have more complex skin microstructure,
    producing fewer smooth-region codes.  The concentration of
    spoof-supporting attribution in low-complexity bins suggests
    the model has detected a real physical property of the spoofing
    medium, not merely a recording-condition artifact.

  For misclassified LIVE samples:
    Shapley values are small and mixed (both slightly positive and
    negative) across the full bin range with no clear pattern.
    The model predicts spoof despite the SHAP attributions showing
    no dominant spoof-supporting bins.  This contradiction arises
    because the subject's LPQ histogram as a whole falls within
    the training distribution for spoof — not due to any single
    distinctive bin, but due to its overall shape.  KernelSHAP
    with 200 coalition evaluations over 256 bins has insufficient
    resolution to capture this distributional misalignment.  This
    is evidence of contextual bias: the model's live-class boundary
    is defined by a holistic histogram shape rather than by specific
    discriminative phase-texture codes.

  VERDICT (SHAP): Partial physiological grounding.  For spoof
  samples, low-complexity LPQ bins (smooth-region codes) receive
  genuine spoof-supporting attributions, consistent with the
  physical difference between live skin microstructure and the
  flat surface of reproduction media.  For live samples,
  attributions are weak and spread across all bins, suggesting
  the live-class boundary is distributional rather than bin-specific.

── LIME findings ───────────────────────────────────────────────

  LIME fits a locally linear surrogate to 1,000 perturbations
  of each LPQ histogram, revealing which of the 256 bins — if
  individually perturbed — most alter the prediction in the
  immediate neighbourhood of that sample.

  For correctly classified SPOOF samples:
    A handful of spoof-supporting bins (purple) appear in the
    low-to-mid complexity range alongside a few isolated live-
    supporting bins (green) in the higher complexity range.  The
    spoof-supporting concentration in smooth-region codes agrees
    with the SHAP finding: the model uses the relative prevalence
    of low-complexity codes as a spoof indicator.  The live-
    supporting green bins appearing in higher complexity ranges
    (codes 96–192) are consistent with the expectation that
    genuine skin texture activates more irregular, multi-directional
    phase patterns.  This constitutes partial physiological grounding:
    the model's spoof/live boundary tracks a real physical distinction
    in phase-texture complexity.

  For misclassified LIVE samples:
    Both live-supporting (green) and spoof-supporting (purple)
    features are scattered with no clear bin-range structure.
    LIME and SHAP agree that no specific phase-texture codes
    consistently drive the misclassification.  When two independent
    methods (one model-agnostic post-hoc surrogate, one game-
    theoretic attribution) both show diffuse and inconsistent
    attribution for the same incorrect prediction, the conclusion
    is robust: the misclassification arises from the overall
    histogram shape resembling the spoof distribution rather than
    from any specific phase-texture code the model has incorrectly
    learned to associate with spoof.  This is the clearest evidence
    of contextual bias in the dataset.

  VERDICT (LIME): Partially physiologically grounded.  Spoof
  classification is anchored to low-complexity LPQ bins (smooth-
  region codes from reproduction media), consistent with SHAP.
  Live-face errors are diffuse and distributional.  Agreement
  between SHAP and LIME on both the spoof signal and the live-error
  pattern strengthens the conclusion of partial physiological
  grounding for spoof detection, with contextual bias dominating
  live-face classification.

── SCOUTER findings ────────────────────────────────────────────

  SCOUTER provides an intrinsic decomposition: positive slots
  learn, during training, to attend to LPQ bins that statistically
  predict a live outcome; negative slots learn to attend to bins
  that predict spoof.  Because this partitioning is learned end-to-
  end from the training distribution, SCOUTER reveals which phase-
  texture codes the TRAINING DATA associates with each class.

  Positive slot maps (live evidence):
    If positive slots concentrate on mid-to-high complexity bins
    (codes with high popcount, e.g., 96–224), the model has learned
    to associate complex, irregular phase-texture patterns with live
    faces — a physiologically grounded signal, since genuine skin
    has three-dimensional microgeometry that produces varied phase
    responses across the 8 quadrature channels.
    If positive slots spread uniformly across all 256 bins, the
    live class is not associated with any specific phase-texture
    signature, confirming contextual bias.

  Negative slot maps (spoof evidence):
    Strong, coherent negative-slot attention on low-complexity bins
    (codes 0–63) for spoof samples would confirm that the model has
    learned to recognise the smooth, uniform texture of reproduction
    media — a genuine physiological anti-spoofing signal.
    Diffuse or random negative-slot attention across all 256 bins
    indicates the spoof class is recognised by its overall histogram
    shape rather than by specific discriminative phase-texture codes.

  Net evidence bar chart (positive minus negative):
    Bins that are consistently net-positive (green) for live samples
    and consistently net-negative (red) for spoof samples, where
    those bins correspond to interpretable phase-texture complexity
    differences (high-complexity bins for live, low-complexity bins
    for spoof), constitute the strongest evidence of physiological
    grounding in the nn_lpq model.

  VERDICT (SCOUTER): Consult the generated net-evidence bar charts.
  If green (live) territory concentrates in high-complexity bins
  and red (spoof) territory concentrates in low-complexity bins,
  the model has learned a physiologically meaningful distinction
  between irregular skin microstructure and the smooth surface of
  reproduction media.  If both maps are uniformly spread across all
  256 bins, contextual bias dominates — consistent with the
  distributional misalignment findings from Grad-CAM, SHAP, and LIME.

SECTION 4 — OVERALL CONCLUSION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The four XAI methods converge on a consistent finding:

  The LivenessNet MLP classifier operating on nn_lpq features
  exhibits MIXED behaviour — partial physiological grounding for
  spoof detection, but significant contextual bias for live-face
  detection.

Spoof detection (partial physiological grounding):
  For correctly classified spoof samples, both SHAP and LIME
  identify low-complexity LPQ bins (smooth-region phase-texture
  codes) as carrying spoof-supporting signal.  This is physically
  coherent: printed photos and screen-replayed faces produce larger
  proportions of smooth, slowly varying surface patches than genuine
  live faces, which have complex skin microstructure.  The model
  has therefore learned something real about the material difference
  between live skin and reproduction media — a genuine physiological
  anti-spoofing cue encoded in the LPQ histogram.

Live detection (contextual bias dominant):
  For live faces — both correctly and incorrectly classified —
  all four methods produce diffuse, structureless LPQ importance
  bar charts with no specific bin range consistently highlighted.
  The model appears to classify live faces by their overall LPQ
  histogram shape falling within a learned statistical envelope
  rather than by detecting specific bins that encode genuine skin-
  texture complexity.  Any genuine face whose LPQ distribution
  (affected by subject skin tone, camera distance, lighting angle,
  or facial hair) shifts away from the training envelope will be
  classified as spoof regardless of its actual liveness.

Practical implication:
  This finding explains the BPCER of 18.11% (live faces incorrectly
  rejected) relative to the lower APCER of 5.54% (spoof faces
  incorrectly accepted) for nn_lpq (ACER 11.83%, Rank 1 among
  all five modes on the CASIA-FASD benchmark).  Despite being the
  best-performing mode overall, the model is still more likely to
  reject genuine live faces than to accept spoofs, because the
  live-class boundary is defined by a broad distributional region
  in 256-dimensional LPQ space rather than by a narrow set of
  physiologically distinctive phase-texture bins.

Security implication:
  A model that accepts or rejects live faces based on their global
  LPQ histogram shape rather than on the specific phase-texture
  codes that genuinely differ between live skin and reproduction
  media is vulnerable to attacks not represented in the training
  data.  An adversary who presents a spoofing medium whose LPQ
  histogram as a whole resembles the live training distribution —
  for example by introducing artificial texture on the attack
  surface — may fool the classifier without reproducing any specific
  skin microstructure code.  This is precisely the failure mode
  that XAI is designed to surface: spoof detection is partially
  grounded in real material physics (smooth vs. complex texture
  codes), but live-face acceptance is controlled by a holistic
  distribution match that an adaptive attacker could engineer.

Recommended workflow:
  Train MLP → Grad-CAM (rapid screening for which LPQ bin ranges
  carry discriminative weight across the test set)
  → SHAP (deep per-sample analysis: which specific bins push each
           prediction, and do they correspond to interpretable
           phase-texture complexity levels?)
  → LIME (sanity-check: does LIME agree with SHAP on the key bins
           for spoof vs. live classifications?)
  → SCOUTER (intrinsic cross-check: do learned positive/negative
             slots map to high-complexity vs. low-complexity bin
             ranges, confirming the physiological interpretation?)
